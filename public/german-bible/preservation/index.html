01

How do I preserve my books?

The most effective and economical preservation measures are preventive: use appropriate storage, maintain a stable storage environment, and handle books carefully.

If a book needs treatment, consult a qualified conservator—especially when it has financial, historical, or strong sentimental value.

02

Should I wear gloves when handling rare books?

Always handle collection material with clean, dry hands, whether or not you wear gloves.

Clean gloves reduce the transfer of natural skin oils, but they can make page turning harder and reduce tactility. For many paper books, careful handling with freshly washed and thoroughly dried hands presents less physical risk than clumsy handling in gloves.

03

Should I oil my leather books?
Do not oil or “dress” leather bindings. These treatments can stain, weaken, or complicate later conservation.

If leather is powdery, crumbling, stiff, or cracked, place the book in a suitable protective box or consult a conservator. For books that must be handled, a properly made polyester-film jacket can help contain leather dust and prevent transfer to hands and pages.

04

What should I do if my books are damaged?

Conservation treatment is time-consuming and costly when done correctly. For a valuable book that is rarely used, leaving it as-is, boxing it, and improving its storage conditions may be the safest choice.

For a book of personal value with minor damage, carefully researched paper-mending guidance may be appropriate if you accept the risk of further damage. If the book is valuable, deteriorating, frequently handled, or structurally complicated, consult a conservator.

Do not use self-stick tape, including products marketed as “archival.” Focus on safer handling, protective housing, and a better environment.

Can I bring my books to the Library of Congress for conservation?

No. The Library of Congress cannot provide preservation or digitisation services for material that is not part of its collections.

Private owners can seek help from a conservator in private practice or from a regional conservation centre. Professional organisations provide directories searchable by region, speciality, and service.

How should I pack books for storage or shipping?
  • Choose boxes small enough to remain safe and manageable when full.
  • For long-term storage, use acid- and lignin-free boxes.
  • Pack books flat with the largest and heaviest at the bottom, or vertically with their spines down.
  • Centre the weight, pad empty spaces, and do not overpack.
  • Keep packed books in a stable, appropriate storage environment.

08

Can books be stored in a basement, attic, or garage if they are in plastic?
Do not store valuable books in basements, attics, or garages. These spaces commonly have unstable temperatures and humidity, with a greater risk of leaks and flooding.

Polyethylene or polypropylene bags and bins can provide secondary protection against water, but they cannot protect books from environmental extremes. Sealed plastic in a humid location can also increase the risk of mould.

09

What should I do if my books get wet or moldy?
Protect yourself first. Water contaminated by sewage and active mould growth can present health hazards. Follow appropriate safety precautions and do not handle unsafe material without suitable protection.

Begin air-drying books immediately after they become wet or mould is discovered, while controlling room temperature and relative humidity. If newly wet or mouldy books cannot be air-dried within two days, prepare them for freezing.

If only old mould stains remain, maintain relative humidity between 40% and 65% to discourage regrowth and inspect the books regularly.

10

How can I reduce a musty or mildew smell?

If there is active, furry, or dried mould, follow the wet-and-mouldy-books guidance first. Odours generally lessen when books receive air circulation in a cool environment with relative humidity around 35–55%.

  • Expose more of the book to moving air without forcing it flat. Stand a sound hardcover slightly open, or support the covers of a large or flexible book and turn to different sections over several days.
  • A book may be placed in a closed container with activated charcoal or baking soda nearby. Prevent direct contact, and inspect frequently for mould.
  • Brief sunlight exposure may reduce odour, but only when fading, discolouration, and yellowing are acceptable risks.

How do I get rid of book pests?

Prevention is the first line of defence. Keep collection areas clean, uncluttered, and free of food or drink residues. Maintain relative humidity around 35–50%.

Infested books can sometimes be treated by carefully controlled freezing. Follow specialist instructions rather than placing a valuable book directly into a household freezer without preparation.

Are wood bookshelves safe to use?

When buying new storage furniture, powder-coated metal shelving is generally preferable because acids and other compounds from wood can migrate into paper and contribute to deterioration.

Existing wooden shelves can be lined with a stable barrier—such as polyester film, corrugated polyethylene or polypropylene board, metal-foil laminate, acrylic sheet, or glass—to prevent direct contact between books and wood.

How should I clean books and bookshelves?

Begin with good housekeeping: keep books off the floor and replace heating and air-conditioning filters regularly.

Dust books and shelves using dusters that contain no fragrance or added chemicals. A book may be vacuumed with a small, soft-brush hose attachment only when the suction can be reduced. Support fragile material and stop if loose pieces are being disturbed.

What self-stick call-number labels are safe?

The Library of Congress publishes specifications for pressure-sensitive call-number labels used on book spines and boxes. For a valuable or fragile book, avoid attaching a label directly when a non-invasive alternative will work.

Two alternatives are:

  • Place the book in a protective box and attach the label to the box.
  • Insert a 2-inch-wide cardstock tab inside the front cover. Make the tab about ⅜ inch taller than the book and place the call number at the top so it remains visible on the shelf.
